02 · The recording

One local field potential, several rhythms.

An LFP is a continuous electrical signal recorded near a population of neurons. Separating it by frequency is like tuning one receiver to several overlapping stations.

The LFP aggregates transmembrane currents over a local volume. Frequency-resolved power can therefore carry mixtures of local spiking-linked activity, synaptic input, and broader network dynamics.

Tune the receiver

High gamma · 70–150 Hz

Fast fluctuations often track local population spiking more closely.

The published intervention tests whether high-gamma CP depends on intact local spiking output.

Frequency is not a source label. The intervention—not the band name alone—provides leverage on where the choice-related component originates.

03 · The method

Can band-limited power predict the eventual choice?

Choice probability asks how often an ideal observer could guess the choice from one neural measurement. A value of 0.5 means chance; larger values mean the two choice groups separate.

For each matched stimulus condition, trials are divided by choice and ranked by band-limited LFP power. ROC area estimates the probability that a randomly drawn trial from one choice group exceeds one from the other.

04 · The intervention

Silence local spikes. Ask which choice signals survive.

Muscimol locally silenced spiking output in visual cortex while leaving measurable LFP activity. A surviving choice signal therefore cannot require the local spikes that were removed.

Local muscimol inactivation abolished spiking in MT or V4 while preserving frequency-resolved LFP recordings. Comparing pre- and post-inactivation CP tests dependence on local spiking output; it does not, by itself, identify a unique remote source.

05 · The insight

Frequency separates signals with different dependencies.

The choice-related activity was not one voice carried at several pitches. Fast and slower rhythms behaved differently across time and under local silencing.

The interaction between frequency, task epoch, and inactivation argues against treating broadband LFP CP as a unitary signal. It supports distinct local-spiking-linked and locally independent components.

Which conclusion survives the causal test?

A strong analysis separates what the data show from what they merely suggest. Test the inferential boundary of this experiment.

Choose the most defensible claim.

The best answer should explain both disappearance and persistence without naming a source the intervention did not isolate.

07 · The broader method

Use correlation to find a signal. Use intervention to constrain its source.

The project offers a reusable logic for studying distributed brain processes—provided the behavioral comparison, neural measurement, and causal manipulation answer distinct parts of the question.

1

Match the evidence

Compare trials that share the relevant stimulus or task condition, so the neural difference tracks the internal outcome rather than an external confound.

2

Decode without overclaiming

ROC/AUC quantifies association. It shows how well a measurement distinguishes outcomes, not whether that signal caused the outcome.

3

Perturb a candidate source

Remove one mechanism and ask what vanishes, what persists, and which alternative explanations remain.

This logic can be adapted to questions about perception, attention, memory, and confidence. Each application still needs task-specific controls and an intervention that targets the proposed mechanism.
